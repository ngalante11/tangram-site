Tangram vs. Chalkie: Which AI Lesson Planning Platform Is Better for Teachers?

AI lesson planning platforms continue to reshape how teachers prepare instruction, helping educators create engaging lessons while reducing planning time. Tangram and Chalkie both use artificial intelligence to support classroom planning, but they were designed to solve different instructional challenges. Understanding those differences can help educators choose the platform that best matches their planning needs.

Lesson Planning Philosophy

Tangram approaches lesson planning as a process that starts with the class rather than the content. Every lesson moves through the Shared Space Teaching (SST) methodology, settling the objective, engagement, instructional strategies, independent practice, assessment, and reflection before anything is generated. Those questions are less about the curriculum, which most teachers already have, than about the room the lesson has to work in.

Chalkie focuses on generating curriculum-aligned lessons, slides, worksheets and activities from a topic, a pasted plan, an uploaded file, a web page or a YouTube video, aligned to the UK National Curriculum, the Australian Curriculum, Common Core or NGSS. Its AI emphasizes speed and ease of content creation for everyday classroom use.

Planning Workflow

The largest difference lies in how each platform supports teachers after lesson generation.

Chalkie enables educators to rapidly create instructional content and then refine or edit lessons as needed using AI.

Tangram begins with class composition. Teachers create Accommodation Profiles once, and the platform automatically generates distinct lesson versions for every class period while maintaining the same instructional objective.

Lesson Architecture

Tangram incorporates instructional coaching throughout the planning process. Rather than simply generating content, the platform prompts teachers to consider cognitive demand, student misconceptions, instructional strategy selection, assessment alignment, and student agency before finalizing each lesson. Each prompt is attached to the section being planned, so the test is whether the lesson works for these students.

Chalkie emphasizes producing polished instructional materials efficiently, making it easy for teachers to move from idea to classroom-ready resources in a short amount of time.

Expert Summary

Both platforms help teachers save valuable planning time.

Chalkie excels at quickly generating lessons, activities, and presentation materials that support curriculum delivery.

Tangram's difference is what the lesson gets aligned to. A curriculum does not change between second period and fifth period. The roster does, and Tangram plans each period against the learners actually in it.

Conclusion

Teachers looking for fast AI-generated lesson materials may appreciate Chalkie's streamlined workflow. Educators who want a plan built for the thirty specific students in fourth period, not for a generic class on that standard, may find Tangram the closer fit.
